# Draft Game Integration

Essentially, tip the player that a collision cascade has begun in orbit, and some pieces are falling to the planet. Satellites scheduled for automated de-orbit are making the situation much worse, and since the planet is doomed anyway, testing is happening to deliberately cause cascades and collisions. Failure is not an option, so they need to pick up the pace to avoid any trouble. Perhaps place some lights in the distance once in a while, representing metal burning in atmosphere? (planet could have thin atmosphere so burning may not make sense.)

Some time later, when the player is travelling between locations (however that works?) put lights in the sky and a few moments later have the mission leader tell them "move, a collision just put large fragments into the atmosphere at a shallow angle, they're going to hit your position." Or, rather, because technology is good, they get like a three minute forecast. The fragments could reach the surface in less than a minute but the collision is predicted early. Basically, they are large enough and slow enough to not fully disintegrate. Fragments that have trailing smoke but you can see them when they get close. I think thin atmosphere makes sense, it isn't a fire ball it is a big space satellite with solar panels growing larger in the sky and there's a lot.

Player has to take cover somewhere, like the side of a crater or in a deep canyon. Buildings don't make sense unless there's a bunker, the fragments are fast enough that a collision is like a liquid impact, concrete would vaporize and the ground turns to sand.

# The Dream As I Remember It

This was the dream and it was pretty wicked and unsettling and that's what I want to convey:

It was dark or dusk, I could see things that look like the hubble telescope in the sky and they were clearly approaching me. My brother was there with me, it was obvious things were pretty grim. Like seeing a meteor and knowing you just have to hope nothing hits you. We had seconds to react. Ran into concrete buildings and looking out windows facing perpendicular to the direction the debris comes from, satellites hitting the ground on fire, houses being replaced with smoke. Small pieces, too small to see, impacting walls like hail. You can see holes in the ground appear like there was a giant shotgun in the sky. Concrete pieces fly across the rooms and the sound was terrible. This is when I decided to wake up and end it.
